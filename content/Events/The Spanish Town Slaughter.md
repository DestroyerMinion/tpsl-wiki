Spanish Town had become the capital of Jamaica in 1692 after an earthquake left the previous capital Port Royal devastated. It quickly become the economical, political, and judicial core of the English presence in the Caribbean, while its predecessor Port Royal devolved further and further into anarchy and a haven for the desperate and cruel. It was considered a fortress and locals felt safe in the knowledge that neither pirates nor other European powers would dare stage an assault against the English seat of Caribbean power.

In September, just a few months ago, the slowly building of the wind to a roar and darkening sky announced the approach of a seasonal hurricane. Nothing out of the ordinary to the locals. They battened down the hatches, retreated to the comfort of their homes or a local inn and waited for it all to blow over. The eyewall of the hurricane, the most violent part of the storm that forms a barrier around the calm eye of the storm, reached the town and eager locals started to flood out into the streets. Even to the storm weather locals, the eye of a storm granting a reprieve and a unique experience was something not many of them had experienced before. A final relentless blast of wind rattled the walls and uprooted a few bricks from the paved roads, and then doors were thrown open!

The people were greeted by a clear blue sky, and a pleasantly warm breeze, that carried the approaching fleet of roughly 20 pirate ships right into Spanish Town's harbor. They had used the eye of the storm to conceal themselves, sailing right along the inner eyewall so that they could swarm the town the instant the eye reached the town. Details of what followed are scarce and unreliable. Survivors of the event rarely speak of what happened, apart from mentioning the relief the other half of the storm brought from the incessant screams emanating from the mayors manor that [[The Fury]] occupied soon after descending.

Due to the storm, it took several days before reports started to spread and English reinforcements could be dispatched to the town to reclaim it. There was an attempt to suppress the exact details of what was found, but drunk and frightened soldiers seeking comfort in the arms of sex workers quickly spilled vague details. Buildings had been set to the torch often with inhabitants still inside; most survivors were found in the forests outside the city limits, having preferred to brave the storm rather than The Fury. The inside of the mayors house saw several veteran soldiers turn tail, the inside resembling scenes straight out of the Eighth Circle of Hell in the aftermath of The Furys brief stay. Different rooms in the manor seemed to contain different groups of people, all executed by The Fury in various brutal ways. Most were to scarred by the experience to reveal specifics.

The Fury herself and her fleet disappeared from the Caribbean, and speculation was abound that she was a servant of the Devil appearing and disappearing at will executing their plan. It was not until a month after the Slaughter that the fleet was spotted in the Bahamas, an area that The Fury had previously never showed an interest in, having spent all her previous resources and time on her influence in the Caribbean.

Was she just fleeing the English revenge? Or was there another reason for her sudden change of hunting grounds...
